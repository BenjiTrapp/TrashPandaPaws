# The new raccoon logo in the C2 — design

Date: 2026-11-17
Size: a small change

## Intent

The operator console should carry the new artwork — the raccoon in the
beret holding a glowing USB stick — as both the header logo and the
browser-tab favicon, in place of the face cropped out of the wallpaper.

The artwork is `logo.gif`: 443x394, 8 frames at 180 ms, looping. The
eyes, the USB stick, the beret badge and the red rim light pulse from
dim (frame 0) to bright (frame 4) and back. The background is
transparent; GIF transparency is binary, so the outline is hard-edged
and only looks smooth once downscaled.

## Goals

- The header shows the whole motif, animating, without distortion.
- The tab icon animates in every browser the operator might use.
- The favicon's animation is the artwork's own, not one invented in
  JavaScript.
- The committed assets stay derivable from one source, and a test still
  says so.

## Non-goals

- Re-theming the console. Only the logo and the favicon change.
- Touching `static/trashpanda_logo.png` or the `/logo.png` route, which
  exist for anything that already linked them.
- Smoothing or redrawing the artwork. It is used as delivered.

## Anti-goals

- A tab icon that is animated in Firefox and frozen on the dimmest
  frame everywhere else.
- A second copy of the 683 kB GIF in the repository, free to drift from
  the first.
- Keeping the synthetic eye-glow animation on top of artwork that
  already glows, which would be two pulses out of phase.

## Constraints

- `tools/make_brand_assets.py` generates the committed assets and may
  use Pillow (a declared development dependency).
  `software/tests/test_brand_assets.py` deliberately may not: it carries
  its own PNG reader so the suite can check the pixels without Pillow.
  That reader handles 8-bit RGB only, and the new assets need an alpha
  channel.
- The operator page serves `/static/<path>` out of
  `software/c2/static/`. The brand source lives in `static/`.
- Favicon animation runs for as long as the console is open, so it must
  keep backing off while the tab is hidden and must not retry a failing
  canvas every frame.

## Approaches considered

1. **Serve `logo.gif` as the favicon directly.** Simplest: two `<link>`
   changes and the glow animation deleted. Rejected — only Firefox
   animates GIF favicons; Chrome and Safari would show frame 0, the
   dimmest, as a still.
2. **Keep the JavaScript glow, recalibrated.** Render a still PNG chain
   from the brightest frame and move `FAVICON_EYES` onto the new eyes.
   Rejected — it paints an invented pulse over artwork that pulses
   already, and keeps a calibration that has to be maintained.
3. **Cycle the artwork's own frames onto the favicon.** Chosen. The
   generator writes the 8 frames as one 512x64 strip; the page slices
   cells into a 64x64 canvas at the GIF's own 180 ms and sets them as
   the icon. Works in every browser, needs no calibration, and the
   animation is the artist's.

Chosen with the full motif in the header (not the round crop, which cuts
off the beret and the hands) and the full motif in the tab, both picked
by the operator from a rendered comparison.

## What gets built

**Source of truth**: `static/logo.gif`, committed. Served to the page by
a `/logo.gif` route next to the existing `/logo.png`, so the 683 kB file
is not duplicated into `software/c2/static/`.

**Generated by `tools/make_brand_assets.py`** (reworked: source becomes
the GIF, the wallpaper crop and the eye-finding go away):

| Asset | What it is |
|---|---|
| `static/logo_256.png` | the still master: brightest frame, padded square, one Lanczos reduction |
| `static/logo_512.png` | Lanczos upscale of the squared 443 px still frame (one resample of the artwork's own pixels, not of the 256 px master), for slides and READMEs; 443 px is all the detail there is |
| `software/c2/static/favicon-{128,64,32,16}.png` | the still at the sizes the page links; 64 is written as the strip's bright cell, so the tab's static icon and the animation cannot drift |
| `software/c2/static/favicon-frames-64.png` | 512x64 strip, the 8 frames at 64px, in order |

All of them RGBA, each one premultiplied-Lanczos-reduced from the squared
frame. The old pipeline could claim "every size is an exact 2x2 average
of the one above" because its source was already 256 px; this source is
443 px, so a reduction is unavoidable and the chain claim goes with it.
What replaces it: `--check` re-derives every committed asset from the
committed GIF and reports `DIFFERS` for any that has been touched, and
the test re-runs it. The hand-derived halving test in
`test_brand_assets.py` is deleted.

`raccoon_face_256.png` and `raccoon_face_512.png` are replaced by
`logo_256.png` and `logo_512.png`; the name no longer describes a face
cropped from a wallpaper.

**`software/c2/server.py`**:

- the header `<img>` takes `/logo.gif`, with `object-fit:contain`, no
  `border-radius`, and a red `drop-shadow` in place of the ring
- a `/logo.gif` route
- `animateFavicon()` rewritten to cycle the strip's cells: same hidden-tab
  back-off, same `onerror` leaving the static icon alone, same
  give-up-once on a `toDataURL` throw
- `FAVICON_EYES` and `FAVICON_EYE_RADIUS` deleted
- the static `<link>`s keep pointing at the PNGs, so the tab is right
  before any script runs

**`software/tests/test_brand_assets.py`**:

- its PNG reader gains colour type 6
- the exact-halving class is replaced by one that re-runs the generator's
  `--check`: every committed asset still matches what the GIF produces,
  and an asset edited by hand is reported as differing
- the glow-geometry class is replaced by one on the strip: it is 8 cells
  of 64x64, consecutive cells differ, the cells' total red rises then
  falls (the pulse), and the brightest cell is byte-identical to
  `favicon-64.png`
- the header class asserts the image cannot be distorted — `contain` on
  a non-square source — rather than that its source is square
- the node harness drives the new loop: it loads the strip, keeps
  replacing the icon, walks the cells in order, and draws nothing while
  the tab is hidden

## Left to the builder

Exact CSS numbers for the header (box size, glow radius and colour),
the strip's PNG encoding details, and how the frame cursor is kept
(index and timer) are the builder's, as long as the timing is the GIF's
own 180 ms and the existing back-off and failure behaviour survive.
